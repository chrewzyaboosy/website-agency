    How it works

    Detectors don't read meaning. They read patterns.

    AI detectors look at how something is written: how predictable each word is, how evenly the sentences flow, and which stock phrases show up. Inkling changes exactly those patterns and leaves your message alone.

    1. Perplexity: less predictable wording

    Language models pick the most likely next word, again and again. That makes their writing smooth but very easy to predict, which is the first thing detectors measure. Inkling swaps in word choices and sentence openers that a person would plausibly use but a model rarely picks.

    2. Burstiness: a human rhythm

    People write in bursts. A long, winding sentence, then a short one. Like this. AI text tends to keep every sentence about the same length and shape. Inkling splits, merges and reshapes sentences so the rhythm varies the way real writing does.

    3. Fingerprints: the tells, removed

    "Delve into." "A rich tapestry." "In today's fast-paced world." "It's important to note." Openers like Moreover and Furthermore, piles of em dashes, and everything in neat groups of three. Inkling finds these tells and rewrites them, then adds the small things people do: contractions, asides and the odd rhetorical question.

    What stays exactly the same

    Numbers, prices, dates, names, URLs, email addresses, hashtags and your paragraph breaks are locked before rewriting starts. The engine runs as plain code inside this page: no AI model, no server, no upload.

    An honest note: the AI score above is Inkling's own local estimate. Detectors such as GPTZero, ZeroGPT and Originality.ai use their own private models and change often. Inkling is built to score as human on them, but nobody can guarantee a specific result, so check the detector you care about and re-roll a variant if needed.

    FAQ

    Questions marketers ask us

    Will Inkling pass GPTZero, ZeroGPT and other detectors?

    It's built for exactly that. Inkling targets the signals those tools measure (predictability, sentence rhythm and stock AI phrasing), and most drafts score as human after a Balanced or Aggressive pass. That said, detectors are private, imperfect and updated often, so no tool can honestly promise a result on every text. If a detector still flags something, hit New variant or step up the strength.

    Is my text uploaded or stored anywhere?

    No. The rewriting engine is ordinary code that runs inside this web page. Your text is never sent to a server, and Inkling only remembers your settings (tone, strength, spelling), never your text.

    Will it change my facts, numbers or links?

    It's designed not to. Numbers, prices, dates, names, URLs, emails, hashtags and paragraph breaks are protected before rewriting. Like any editing tool, though, it can occasionally shift nuance, so give the result a quick read before you publish.

    What's the difference between Light, Balanced and Aggressive?

    Light fixes the obvious AI tells and adds contractions, keeping your wording close to the original. Balanced also reworks rhythm and phrasing and suits most marketing copy. Aggressive restructures more sentences for the strongest effect on detectors, with a bit more distance from the original wording.

    Which tone should I choose?

    Natural is a safe default. Casual suits social posts and friendly emails, Professional suits B2B and reports, Marketing adds punch for ads and landing pages, and Academic keeps a measured, formal register.

    Why is Inkling's AI score different from my detector's?

    Inkling's score is a transparent local estimate built from a handful of well-known signals, so you can see why a text looks machine-written. Commercial detectors use their own trained models. Treat our score as a guide, and check with the detector your client or platform actually uses.

    Does it work in other languages?

    Inkling is tuned for English (US and UK spelling) today. Other languages may pass through with only light changes.

    Does it work offline?

    Yes. Once the page has loaded, everything runs locally. There's also a single-file version you can save and open straight from your computer.

    Is it OK to use Inkling?

    Inkling is for polishing marketing copy that you have the right to publish. Don't use it to break academic integrity rules, an employer's or client's AI policy, or a platform's disclosure requirements. You're responsible for checking facts and claims in anything you publish.
